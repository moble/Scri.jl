BMS Action on Fields

Having worked out how the tetrad transforms, we can read off how the field components built from it transform. Every asymptotic component is a contraction of a curvature spinor (or tensor) with some number of tetrad legs, so its transformation is assembled from three ingredients, each of which we already have:

  1. a conformal weight $κ^w$ — one factor of $κ^{-1/2}$ for each basis spinor in the contraction, plus the weight of the underlying field;
  2. a spin phase $e^{i s γ}$ for a component of spin weight $s$, from the $K$ (Hopf-fiber) rotation by $γ$ about the null direction — this is the ordinary spin-weighted rotation of a field on the sphere (by the inverse of the frame rotation; see Frames and Components), and in practice is encoded in the rotation/aberration of the angular grid rather than applied component-by-component;
  3. a component mixing generated by the null rotation, with the same parameter that shears the dyad.

Throughout, $b$ denotes that null-rotation parameter — the coefficient of the generator in the transformed dyad.[b] From the dyad transformation,

\[m̃' = \frac{e^{iγ}}{κ}\left(m̃ + b\, ñ\right), \qquad b = \frac{ðu'}{2κ} \quad\text{at } ℐ⁺.\]

Weyl components

The Newman–Penrose Weyl components are the contractions (see Tensor Components)

\[\begin{aligned} ψ_0 &= C_{abcd}\, ℓ^a m^b ℓ^c m^d, & ψ_1 &= C_{abcd}\, ℓ^a n^b ℓ^c m^d, & ψ_2 &= C_{abcd}\, ℓ^a m^b \bar m^c n^d, \\ ψ_3 &= C_{abcd}\, ℓ^a n^b \bar m^c n^d, & ψ_4 &= C_{abcd}\, n^a \bar m^b n^c \bar m^d, && \end{aligned}\]

with spin weights $+2, +1, 0, -1, -2$ and — as we now show — a common conformal weight $κ^{-3}$.

Conformal weight

In spinor form $ψ_n$ is the Weyl spinor $ψ_{ABCD}$ contracted with four basis spinors (the spinor "square roots" of $ℓ$ and $n$). Because every tetrad leg varies by the same factor $1/κ$, the inverse of the conformal factor (the result $ĝ' = κ²ĝ$ from the tetrad page), each basis spinor varies by $κ^{-1/2}$, and the four of them contribute $κ^{-2}$. The asymptotic Weyl spinor itself is the rescaled, finite-at-$ℐ$ field $ψ = Ψ/ω$ built from the physical $Ψ$; since $ω' = κω$ and $Ψ$ is coordinate-independent, $ψ$ contributes one further $κ^{-1}$. The total is

\[ψ_n \longmapsto κ^{-3} \times (\text{spin phase}) \times (\text{mixing}),\]

the same $κ^{-3}$ for all five components.

Mixing: the peeling tower

The mixing is a null rotation fixing the generator $ñ$ with parameter $b$. Acting on the Weyl tensor through its tetrad legs, this is the textbook null-rotation tower

\[ψ_n' = κ^{-3}\sum_{k=0}^{4-n} \binom{4-n}{k}\, b^{\,k}\, ψ_{n+k}, \qquad b = \frac{ðu'}{2κ},\]

up to the spin phase. Written out,

\[\begin{aligned} ψ_4' &= κ^{-3}\, ψ_4, \\ ψ_3' &= κ^{-3}\left(ψ_3 + b\,ψ_4\right), \\ ψ_2' &= κ^{-3}\left(ψ_2 + 2b\,ψ_3 + b^2 ψ_4\right), \\ ψ_1' &= κ^{-3}\left(ψ_1 + 3b\,ψ_2 + 3b^2 ψ_3 + b^3 ψ_4\right), \\ ψ_0' &= κ^{-3}\left(ψ_0 + 4b\,ψ_1 + 6b^2 ψ_2 + 4b^3 ψ_3 + b^4 ψ_4\right). \end{aligned}\]

Only $ψ_4$ — the component assembled entirely from the fixed generator $ñ$ and the screen — is left unmixed; the tower fills in the lower components by successive applications of $b$. Since $b$ grows linearly in $u$ (the lever arm of the null rotation), so does the mixing.

At $ℐ⁻$ the generator is $l̃$ instead of $ñ$, so the null rotation fixes $l̃$ and the tower runs the other way — $ψ_0$ is the unmixed component and the binomial sum builds $ψ_4$ from it, now with the conjugate parameter $\bar b = ð̄v'/2κ$ (see Past null infinity below).

Faraday components

The Maxwell/Faraday components $φ_0, φ_1, φ_2$ are contractions of the Faraday spinor $φ_{AB}$ (rank two) with two basis spinors, with spin weights $+1, 0, -1$. The counting is the same as for the Weyl components but with two legs instead of four: two basis spinors give $κ^{-1}$ and the rescaled field $φ = Φ/ω$ gives another $κ^{-1}$, for a total conformal weight $κ^{-2}$. The null rotation produces the shorter tower

\[\begin{aligned} φ_2' &= κ^{-2}\, φ_2, \\ φ_1' &= κ^{-2}\left(φ_1 + b\,φ_2\right), \\ φ_0' &= κ^{-2}\left(φ_0 + 2b\,φ_1 + b^2 φ_2\right), \end{aligned}\]

with the same reversal at $ℐ⁻$.

Strain, shear, and news

The shear $σ$ is a different kind of object: it is not a curvature tensor contracted with the tetrad but a connection coefficient, and it transforms inhomogeneously under supertranslations. This is the algebraically richest of the transformation laws, and the origin of gravitational memory.

The asymptotic shear $σ$ (spin weight $+2$) measures how a cut of $ℐ⁺$ fails to be a good cut. Relative to a reference foliation, a cut at retarded time $u = f(θ, ϕ)$ has shear

\[σ[f] = σ^0 - \frac{ð^2 f}{2\sqrt2},\]

where $σ^0$ is the shear of the reference cut. The factor has the same dyad/$\sqrt2$ origin as the $½$ in the mixing parameter $b$: the shear contracts two dyad legs into a second derivative, and since $m̃ = (∂_θ + i\cscθ\,∂_ϕ)/\sqrt2$ includes a $1/\sqrt2$ on each leg, $m̃^A m̃^B D_A D_B = \tfrac12 ð^2$ in the Newman–Penrose normalization of $ð$; the remaining $1/\sqrt2$ and the sign come from $b$ itself, as derived below.

This shift comes straight out of the tetrad transformation. Feed the transformed legs

\[m̃' = \frac{e^{iγ}}{κ}\left(m̃ + b\,ñ\right), \qquad l̃' = \frac{1}{κ}\left(l̃ + \bar b\,m̃ + b\,m̄̃ + |b|^2\,ñ\right), \qquad b = \frac{ðu'}{2κ},\]

into the definition, with $∇'$ the covariant derivative compatible with the primed conformal metric $ĝ' = κ²ĝ$:

\[σ' = -c_σ\, m̃'^a m̃'^b ∇'_a l̃'_b.\]

At the default $c_σ = 1$ this definition is Eq. (85c) of Moxon et al. [3] evaluated in the primed frame, so the law derived here is precisely the transformation of the SpECTRE/SXS shear.

The spin–boost prefactors of the three legs, together with $∇'$, combine into the single boost weight the shear has as a connection coefficient at $ℐ$ — the common factor $κ^{-1}$ of the tetrad page, not the naïve three-leg $κ^{-3}$, the difference being exactly the conformal rescaling absorbed by $∇'$ — and the spin-weight-$+2$ phase $e^{2iγ}$ (encoded, as for the Weyl and Faraday components, by the rotation of the angular grid). Pulling those out, the rest is fixed almost entirely by the projector $m̃'^a m̃'^b$. Because $m̃'$ is null and orthogonal to $ñ$ ($m̃·m̃ = ñ·m̃ = 0$), of the four pieces of $l̃'$ it annihilates three:

  • $\bar b\,m̃$ dies on $m̃'^b m̃_b = 0$;
  • $|b|^2\,ñ$ dies on $m̃'^b ñ_b = 0$;
  • only $b\,m̄̃$ survives, through $m̃'^b m̄̃_b = e^{iγ}/κ$.

The $l̃$ term simply rebuilds the original $σ$. So, with the already-extracted weight and phase set aside,

\[σ' = κ^{-1}\Big(σ \;-\; c_σ\, m̃^a m̃^b D_a\big(b\,m̄̃_b\big)\Big),\]

with $D$ the covariant derivative on the cut. This last term — a derivative of the coefficient $b$, not of a tetrad leg — is responsible for the entire inhomogeneity. It is cleanest for a pure supertranslation ($κ = 1$, $γ = 0$), where $b = ðu'/2$ with $u' = u - c_α α$. The $u$-derivative of $b$ then drops out ($∂_u u'$ is angle-only, so $∂_u b = \tfrac12 ð(∂_u u') = 0$, leaving only the homogeneous transport in a general element). For the screen term, recall that $ðf = -\sqrt2\, m̃(f)$ on spin weight zero (see "The Operator ð"), so $b = -c_α ðα/2 = c_α m̃(α)/\sqrt2$. Decomposing the gradient on the cut as $D_b α = m̃(α)\,m̄̃_b + m̄̃(α)\,m̃_b$, the screen term $b\,m̄̃_b = (c_α/\sqrt2)\, m̃(α)\,m̄̃_b$ is $1/\sqrt2$ times the part of $D_b(c_α α)$ that survives contraction with $m̃^b$. Contracting the remaining derivative twice into $m̃$ turns it into the screen Hessian,

\[- c_σ\, m̃^a m̃^b D_a\big(b\,m̄̃_b\big) = - \frac{c_σ c_α}{\sqrt2}\, m̃^A m̃^B D_A D_B α = - c_σ\,c_α\,\frac{ð^2 α}{2\sqrt2},\]

by the identity above. A general element merely reinstates the boost and spin already pulled out front, so at the defaults $c_σ = c_α = 1$

\[σ' = κ^{-1}\left(σ - \frac{ð^2 α}{2\sqrt2}\right).\]

For a pure supertranslation, where $κ = 1$ and $u' = u - α$, this agrees with Eqs. (40c) and (86c) of Moxon et al. [3]: the former gives $J'^{(1)} = J^{(1)} + ð^2 u'$ for the Bondi metric function $J$, and the latter gives $σ = J^{(1)}/2\sqrt2$ asymptotically. Eq. (40c) has no factor of $κ$, so it confirms only that case, not the general law.

The convention constant $c_σ$ and the time-law sign $c_α$ ride along untouched — the former through $F_σ$, the latter folded into $α$.

The inhomogeneous $ð^2 α$ is the affine action that makes the shear the Goldstone field of supertranslations: there is no frame in which it vanishes for all cuts at once, and the difference between its values before and after a burst of radiation is the memory.

The strain $h$ (spin weight $-2$) is related to the shear by $σ = h̄/2\sqrt2$ on $ℐ⁺$ (see Tensor Components), so it transforms by the conjugate law with the full coefficient,

\[h' = κ^{-1}\left(h - ð̄^2 α\right),\]

since $α$ is real and $\overline{ð^2 α} = ð̄^2 α$.

The news is the retarded-time derivative $N = ∂_u h$ (spin weight $-2$), up to a convention factor $c_N$ discussed below. A $u$-derivative induces one more factor of $1/κ$ (because $u' = κ u$), so the news has conformal weight $κ^{-2}$. Crucially, the supertranslation shift $ð̄^2 α$ is independent of $u$, so the derivative annihilates it and the news transforms homogeneously:

\[N' = κ^{-2}\, N.\]

This is the precise sense in which the news is the supertranslation-invariant (up to weight) part of the radiation, while the shear is not.

Past null infinity

Every $ℐ⁻$ law follows from the $ℐ⁻$ tetrad, where $l̃$ and $ñ$ exchange roles: $l̃$ is the generator, $ñ$ is transverse, and the advanced time $v$ replaces $u$. The null rotation now fixes $l̃$, shearing the dyad as $m̃ ↦ m̃ + b\,l̃$ with $b = ðv'/2κ$ (spin weight $+1$), exactly as at $ℐ⁺$.

The Weyl tower, though, is built not from $b$ but from its conjugate $\bar b = ð̄v'/2κ$ (spin weight $-1$) — another crucial asymmetry between the two ends. An $ñ$-fixed null rotation ($ℐ⁺$) feeds the parameter $b$ itself into the tower, but an $l̃$-fixed rotation ($ℐ⁻$) feeds $\bar b$. Spin weights force it: with the $l̃$-rich component $ψ_0$ now unmixed, the tower runs downward in spin from $ψ_0$ ($s=+2$) to $ψ_4$ ($s=-2$), and the two terms in each rung — e.g., $ψ_1' = ψ_1 + \bar b\,ψ_0$ — share a spin weight only because $\bar b$ lowers spin:

\[ψ_n' = κ^{-3}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\, \bar b^{\,k}\, ψ_{n-k}, \qquad \bar b = \frac{ð̄v'}{2κ},\]

so $ψ_0' = κ^{-3}ψ_0$ and $ψ_4' = κ^{-3}(ψ_4 + 4\bar b\,ψ_3 + 6\bar b^2 ψ_2 + 4\bar b^3 ψ_1 + \bar b^4 ψ_0)$. The Faraday tower reverses the same way ($φ_0$ unmixed, parameter $\bar b$). These reproduce the $I = -1$ branch of Scri.mix_components! — which applies $conj(ðt'╱2κ)$ — term for term.

The radiative shear on $ℐ⁻$ is $λ = c_λ\, m̄̃^a m̄̃^b ∇_a ñ_b$ (spin weight $-2$; see Tensor Components). Repeating the $ℐ⁺$ computation with $ñ' ∋ \bar b\,m̃/κ$ gives the shift $m̄̃^a m̄̃^b D_a(\bar b\,m̃_b) = ð̄^2 α/2\sqrt2$, with no minus sign because the definition of $λ$ has none. Since $λ = h/2\sqrt2$ on $ℐ⁻$, the strain shift flips sign relative to $ℐ⁺$:

\[λ' = κ^{-1}\left(λ + \frac{ð̄^2 α}{2\sqrt2}\right), \qquad h' = κ^{-1}\left(h + ð̄^2 α\right),\]

again matching the code. The news is unchanged, $N' = κ^{-2}N$.

Convention dependence

The laws above are written in the package's default (SXS + Newman–Penrose $ð$) convention. Data in another convention $X$ relates to the SXS data by the per-component factors of the conventions page — all in export form, $q^{[X]} = F q^{[\mathrm{SXS}]}$ —

\[ψ_n^{[X]} = F_n\, ψ_n^{[\mathrm{SXS}]}, \quad F_n = c_s c_ψ c_R\,(c_l c_m)^{2-n}; \qquad h^{[X]} = F_h\, h^{[\mathrm{SXS}]}, \quad F_h = c_s c_h,\]

and analogously for $φ_n$ (a factor $F_{φ,n} = c_s c_φ\,(c_l c_m)^{1-n}$, two legs instead of four) and the radiative shear ($F_σ$ on $ℐ⁺$, $F_λ$ on $ℐ⁻$; below). A transform that stays in convention $X$ is the composition $\text{convert } X{→}\mathrm{SXS}$, then the SXS law above, then $\text{convert back}$. Whether a parameter survives that round trip depends on whether the law is homogeneous or inhomogeneous.

Convention $X$ may also use its own eth, $ð^{[X]} = c_ð\, ð$, whose conjugate is $ð̄^{[X]} = \bar c_ð\, ð̄$ (see the eth page). The laws below are derived with the Newman–Penrose $ð$, and their final forms are then rewritten with $ð^{[X]}$, so that they can be compared directly with formulas written in convention $X$. Each $ð$ becomes $ð^{[X]}/c_ð$, and each $ð̄$ becomes $ð̄^{[X]}/\bar c_ð$.

Homogeneous laws — the peeling towers. For the Weyl tower,

\[ψ_n'^{[X]} = F_n\, ψ_n'^{[\mathrm{SXS}]} = κ^{-3}\sum_k \binom{4-n}{k}\, b^{\,k}\, \frac{F_n}{F_{n+k}}\, ψ_{n+k}^{[X]}, \qquad \frac{F_n}{F_{n+k}} = (c_l c_m)^{k},\]

so the overall signs $c_s c_ψ c_R$ cancel between numerator and denominator and the tower keeps its exact shape with a rescaled parameter

\[b^{[X]} = c_l\, c_m\, b^{[\mathrm{SXS}]} = c_l\, c_m\, \frac{ðu'}{2κ} = \frac{c_l\, c_m}{c_ð}\, \frac{ð^{[X]}u'}{2κ},\]

precisely the dyad scaling found on the tetrad page. The Faraday tower behaves identically: $F_{φ,n}/F_{φ,n+k} = (c_l c_m)^k$ again, and the overall $c_s c_φ$ cancels. So none of $c_s, c_ψ, c_R, c_φ$ enters a same-convention mixing; only $c_l c_m$ does, through $b$.

On $ℐ⁻$ the tower mixes downward (the parameter is $\bar b = ð̄v'/2κ$, multiplying $ψ_{n-k}$ into $ψ_n$), so the factor ratio inverts: $F_n/F_{n-k} = (c_l c_m)^{-k}$, and

\[\bar b^{[X]} = \frac{\bar b^{[\mathrm{SXS}]}}{c_l\, c_m} = \frac{ð̄^{[X]}v'}{2κ\, \bar c_ð\, c_l\, c_m}.\]

Equivalently, from the tetrad: the $ℐ⁻$ case mixes the $l̃$ leg into $m̄̃$, and $c̄_m/c_l = 1/(c_l c_m)$ for real $c_l$ and unit-modulus $c_m$. Note this is not the conjugate of the $ℐ⁺$ factor — conjugating $c_l c_m$ would leave $c_l$ upstairs.

Inhomogeneous laws — the radiative shear and strain. Here the additive shift has nothing to cancel against, so it induces the full conversion factor. On $ℐ⁺$ the radiative shear is $σ$ (spin weight $+2$); on $ℐ⁻$ it is the distinct coefficient $λ$ (spin weight $-2$), exactly as the radiative Weyl component switches from $ψ_4$ to $ψ_0$:

\[\begin{aligned} σ'^{[X]} &= κ^{-1}\!\left(σ^{[X]} - F_σ\,\frac{ð^2 α}{2\sqrt2}\right) = κ^{-1}\!\left(σ^{[X]} - \frac{F_σ}{c_ð^2}\,\frac{(ð^{[X]})^2 α}{2\sqrt2}\right) &&\text{on } ℐ⁺, \\ λ'^{[X]} &= κ^{-1}\!\left(λ^{[X]} + F_λ\,\frac{ð̄^2 α}{2\sqrt2}\right) = κ^{-1}\!\left(λ^{[X]} + \frac{F_λ}{\bar c_ð^2}\,\frac{(ð̄^{[X]})^2 α}{2\sqrt2}\right) &&\text{on } ℐ⁻, \end{aligned}\]

and the strain shift on either infinity is $h'^{[X]} = κ^{-1}(h^{[X]} ∓ F_h\, ð̄^2 α) = κ^{-1}(h^{[X]} ∓ F_h\, (ð̄^{[X]})^2 α/\bar c_ð^2)$ ($-$ on $ℐ⁺$, $+$ on $ℐ⁻$). The homogeneous pieces ride through untouched; only the inhomogeneous term picks up a factor.

From $σ = -c_σ\,m^a m^b ∇_a l_b$: the tetrad legs are defined as vectors ($m^{a\,[X]} = c_m m^a$, $l^{a\,[X]} = c_l l^a$; see the "Tetrad" conventions page), so the two $m$ legs contribute $c_m^2$ with no metric contraction, the connection is convention-independent, and the lowered $l_b = g_{bc} l^c$ contributes $c_s c_l$ — exactly one power of the metric survives:

\[F_σ = c_s\, c_σ\, c_l\, c_m^2.\]

On $ℐ⁻$, $λ = c_λ\,m̄^a m̄^b ∇_a n_b$ (Moxon/SXS, no minus sign, so $c_λ$ is independent of $c_σ$). The two $m̄$ legs give $c̄_m^2 = 1/c_m^2$, and the lowered $n_b = g_{bc} n^c$ gives $c_s/c_l$ (the $n$ leg scales as $1/c_l$), so

\[F_λ = \frac{c_s\, c_λ}{c_l\, c_m^2}.\]

Note $F_σ$ and $F_λ$ include $c_l^{±1}$ (the radiative shear is built from a real null leg) while $F_h$ does not (the strain comes from the metric perturbation, with no $l$ or $n$ leg) — this is why the code keeps the shear and strain shifts as separate arguments. The news, being $∂_u$ of the strain with its inhomogeneous part annihilated, transforms homogeneously and so is convention-independent up to its $κ^{-2}$ weight; its conversion factor is the strain's times $c_N$, $F_N = c_N F_h$, because $N = c_N ∂_u h$ and the coordinates (including $u$) are shared by every convention.

The eth coefficient $c_ð$. The geometric $b$ and the geometric shift $ð^2 α/2\sqrt2 = m̃^A m̃^B D_A D_B α/\sqrt2$ do not depend on how $ð$ is normalized, and the supertranslation $α$ is just a function on the sphere. The factors of $c_ð$ above therefore change how these quantities are written with $ð^{[X]}$, but not their values. For example, the GHP eth has $c_ð = 1/\sqrt{2}$, so that $ð^2 α = 2(ð_{\mathrm{GHP}})^2 α$, and the shear shift on $ℐ⁺$ becomes $-F_σ (ð_{\mathrm{GHP}})^2 α/\sqrt{2}$.

What the code does. Collecting the survivors, a same-convention transform requires exactly three convention insertions on top of the default computation. All three are included in mix_components!, written exactly as in the laws above, with the factors read from the Conventions included in the DataComponents descriptor. The code computes $ð$ internally with the Newman–Penrose normalization, whatever value of $c_ð$ the Conventions records, so $c_ð$ does not enter into any of these insertions:

  1. the peeling parameter is rescaled by Scri.dyad_factor — the towers are generated by $c_l c_m\, ðu'/2κ$ on $ℐ⁺$ and by $(ð̄v'/2κ)/(c_l c_m)$ on $ℐ⁻$ — driving both the Weyl and Faraday towers;
  2. the radiative-shear shift is $-F_σ\,ð^2α/2\sqrt2$ on $ℐ⁺$ with $F_σ$ from Scri.shear_factor, and $+F_λ\,ð̄^2α/2\sqrt2$ on $ℐ⁻$ with $F_λ$ from Scri.lambda_factor; and
  3. the strain shift is $∓F_h\,ð̄^2α$ with $F_h$ from Scri.strain_factor.

The time-law sign is read from the conventions in transform! itself: $c_α$ is folded into $α$ up front, exactly as the former sign argument was. At the defaults $c_l = 1, c_m = 1, c_σ = c_λ = c_s = 1, c_h = 1, c_α = 1$ every factor is the One singleton, and the insertions compile away to the laws above. Conversion between conventions — the full $F$-factors, not just the transform survivors — is Scri.represent!, built on Scri.conversion_factor.

  • bUsing Newman and Penrose's definition of $ð$ and the standard tetrad, we can derive the value of this parameter as $b = ðu'/2κ$. Boyle [7] quotes it as $-ðu'/κ$, but that uses the $ð$ normalization of Geroch et al. [18] (whereby $ð_{\mathrm{NP}} = \sqrt{2}\,ð_{\mathrm{GHP}}$) and a different tetrad (see the footnote on "Tetrad"). Together, those differences in conventions account for the difference in the mixing parameter.

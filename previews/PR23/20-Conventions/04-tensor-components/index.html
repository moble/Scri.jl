Tensor Components

For simplicity, we will now assume that the tetrad is regular at null infinity. That is, we will interpret the tetrad legs $(l, m, m̄, n)$ on this page as the rescaled versions $(l̃, m̃, m̃̄, ñ)$ defined on the previous page. For the Faraday components below, this is enough to make the components finite — and generally nonzero — at null infinity. The Weyl and strain components require one further power of $ω$: the curvature must be read as $K_{abcd} = ω^{-1} C̃_{abcd}$ (where $C̃$ is the Weyl tensor of the conformal metric), and the strain as the leading coefficient of $r\,h$. We leave those rescalings implicit, because they are shared by all conventions, and so cannot affect the conversion factors that are our only concern on this page.

The two shears defined at the end of this page need no further power of $ω$, but they do need a connection, because they are coefficients of one and not components of a tensor. Written with the rescaled legs and with the covariant derivative of the conformal metric $ĝ$, both are finite at null infinity, and both agree with the shears computed from the physical metric and its own legs: the terms by which the two connections differ are each proportional to $m̃ ⋅ m̃$ or to the inner product of $m̃$ with the leg being differentiated, and both of those vanish, so nothing survives the contraction. The quantity being transformed is therefore the familiar peeling coefficient, $\lim_{r → ∞} r^2 σ$ at $ℐ⁺$ and $\lim_{r → ∞} r^2 λ$ at $ℐ⁻$.

The Newman-Penrose Weyl components are defined as

\[\begin{aligned} ψ_0 &= c_ψ C_{abcd} l^a m^b l^c m^d, \\ ψ_1 &= c_ψ C_{abcd} l^a n^b l^c m^d, \\ ψ_2 &= c_ψ C_{abcd} l^a m^b m̄^c n^d, \\ ψ_3 &= c_ψ C_{abcd} l^a n^b m̄^c n^d, \\ ψ_4 &= c_ψ C_{abcd} n^a m̄^b n^c m̄^d. \end{aligned}\]

Combining the Weyl-tensor conversion ${}^{[A]}C_{abcd} = c_s c_R C_{abcd}$ from the previous pages with the leg scalings $c_l$, $1/c_l$, $c_m$, and $c̄_m = 1/c_m$, we can convert between conventions according to the formula

\[{}^{[A]}ψ_n = c_ψ c_s c_R (c_l c_m)^{2-n}\, ψ_n.\]

The (Maxwell/Faraday) field-strength tensor $F_{ab}$ is similarly decomposed into components as

\[\begin{aligned} φ_0 &= c_φ F_{ab}\, l^a m^b, \\ φ_1 &= c_φ \tfrac{1}{2} F_{ab}\, (l^a n^b + m̄^a m^b), \\ φ_2 &= c_φ F_{ab}\, m̄^a n^b. \end{aligned}\]

With $c_φ = 1$ these are the definitions of Eq. (A1) of Newman and Penrose [8] and Eq. (1.1) of Teukolsky [9], both written in the opposite signature. In our signature, they are the definitions of Eq. (8) of Bozzola and Paschalidis [10], and those of $φ_1$ and $φ_2$ in Eqs. (3.3) and (3.4) of Zilhão et al. [11]; both papers write $k$ for our $n$.

These definitions are consistent with the Weyl components above: each $φ_n$ has spin weight $1-n$ (just as $ψ_n$ has $2-n$), and each step down either tower trades a leg scaling as $c_l$ or $c_m$ for one scaling as its inverse.

Unlike the Weyl tensor, $F_{ab}$ is not built from the metric, but its sign still depends on the signature, because the field that all authors share is not $F_{ab}$ itself. Every author writes the Lorentz force on a charge $q$ of mass $m$ in the same form,

\[\frac{du^a}{dτ} = \frac{q}{m} F^a{}_b\, u^b,\]

as in Eq. (3.4) of Misner et al. [5], which calls it the definition of $F^a{}_b$, and in Eq. (23.4) of Landau and Lifschitz [12]. The four-velocity and the proper time are the same in every convention, so $F^a{}_b$ is too, and lowering its index gives

\[{}^{[A]}F_{ab} = {}^{[A]}g_{ac} F^c{}_b = c_s F_{ab}.\]

This is why $F_{0i} = -E_i$ in Eq. (3.7) of Misner et al. [5], whose signature is ours, while $F_{0i} = +E_i$ in Eq. (11.138) of Jackson [13] and Eq. (23.5) of Landau and Lifschitz [12], whose signature is the opposite. More generally, Appendix A of Dreiner et al. [14] shows that contravariant vectors, mixed tensors, and spinors are unchanged by the choice of signature, while each lowered index changes sign. Combining this with the leg scalings, the inter-convention factor is

\[{}^{[A]}φ_n = c_s c_φ\, (c_l c_m)^{1-n}\, φ_n,\]

with the dyad scaling $c_l c_m$ raised to the spin weight $1-n$. The factor $c_s$ arises exactly as it does for the Weyl components, from one lowered index, but there is no Riemann-sign factor, because no curvature enters the definition of $F^a{}_b$.

The same conclusion holds in the language of spinors. The electromagnetic spinor $φ_{AB}$ of Eq. (5.1.39) of Penrose and Rindler [15] is determined by $F^a{}_b$, and so does not depend on the signature, whereas the components $φ_n$ defined above change sign with $c_s$. For a fixed sign $c_R$ of the Riemann tensor, the Weyl spinor and the Weyl components behave in the same way, so in either signature the two towers are related to their spinors by the same sign.

In the default convention, and with the tetrad of the previous page before rescaling, the Coulombic component is $φ_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}(E_{r̂} + i B_{r̂})$, in terms of the orthonormal radial components of the electric and magnetic fields. A positive charge $Q$ therefore has $φ_1 = Q/(2r^2)$, and the rescaled value at null infinity is $Q/2$. The same relation appears as Eq. (3.5) of Zilhão et al. [11] and Eq. (D4b) of Bozzola and Paschalidis [10]. Some authors contract $F_{ab}$ with the legs in the other order, as in $φ_2 = F_{ab} n^a m̄^b$ of Eq. (61) of Palenzuela et al. [16], which corresponds to $c_φ = -1$ if the legs are otherwise the same as ours.

The metric perturbation is defined as

\[h_{ab} = g_{ab} - η_{ab},\]

where $η_{ab}$ is the Minkowski metric. The strain components are defined as

\[\begin{aligned} h_+ &= \frac{1}{2} (h_{θ̂θ̂} - h_{ϕ̂ϕ̂}), \\ h_× &= h_{θ̂ϕ̂}, \end{aligned}\]

where the hats indicate orthonormal components in the spherical basis. These are combined into a single complex strain component[1]

\[h = c_h(h_+ - i h_×).\]

We assume Bondi gauge near null infinity, and the angular part of the metric is

\[r^2 h_{AB} = r^2 \left(γ_{AB} + \frac{C_{AB}}{r} + …\right).\]

Here, $r$ is the areal radius, $γ_{AB}$ is the unit round sphere, and $C_{AB}$ the trace-free "shear tensor" (its time-derivative being the "News tensor", though each is a tensor in a very limited sense). The radiative strain is then the limit implied at the top of this page,

\[h = c_h \lim_{r → ∞} r\,(h_+ - i h_×) = \frac{c_h}{2}\, C_{AB}\, q̄^A q̄^B,\]

where $q^A = \sqrt{2}\, m̃^A / c_m$ is the dyad of the previous page with its convention factor divided out. The dyad appears twice here, so its intrinsic sign is irrelevant.

Alternatively, we could define the strain in terms of the tetrad legs, as

\[h = \frac{c_h}{c̄_m^2} h_{ab} m̄^a m̄^b;\]

the $c̄_m^2$ in the denominator exactly cancels the scaling of the two $m̄$ legs, so the two definitions agree identically. In either case, the only convention factors that survive are $c_h$ itself and the signature flip of the metric perturbation, ${}^{[A]}h_{ab} = c_s h_{ab}$ (both $g_{ab}$ and $η_{ab}$ flip with the signature), so we have

\[{}^{[A]}h = c_s c_h\, h.\]

The News is the time derivative of the strain, up to one more convention factor:

\[N = c_N\, ∂_u h,\]

where $u$ is the retarded time on $ℐ⁺$, and the advanced time $v$ takes its place on $ℐ⁻$. The SXS convention has $c_N = 1$. But Boyle [7] defines the news as $n = -∂_u σ̄$ (Eq. 22 of the arXiv version), which is minus the time derivative of its strain $h = σ̄$, so $c_N = -1$ there. The time coordinates are the same in every convention, so the News converts as the strain does, times $c_N$:

\[{}^{[A]}N = c_N c_s c_h\, N.\]

Near future null infinity, the radiative parts of these fields are related. In the default convention, the relation takes the familiar form

\[ψ_4 ∼ -ḧ,\]

where the dots denote derivatives with respect to the retarded time $u$, and both sides are understood as the finite radiative data described at the top of this page. The relation is itself convention-dependent: dividing the conversion factors above gives

\[{}^{[A]}ψ_4 ∼ -\frac{c_ψ c_R}{c_h (c_l c_m)^2}\, {}^{[A]}ḧ\]

(the $c_s$ factors cancel).

Finally, we also define the shear on $ℐ⁺$ as

\[σ = -c_σ m^a m^b ∇_a l_b,\]

which has spin weight $+2$. With the default $c_σ = 1$ this is Eq. (85c) of Moxon et al. [3], the definition used by the SpECTRE CCE code that produces SXS shear data. The conversion factor follows the same reasoning as above: the two $m$ legs are vectors, contributing $c_m^2$; the covariant derivative is convention-independent (the Christoffel symbols do not change, as shown under "Metric and Curvature"); and $l_b = g_{bc} l^c$ is the lowered tetrad vector, contributing $c_s c_l$ — one power of the metric survives. So

\[{}^{[A]}σ = c_s c_σ c_l c_m^2\, σ.\]

On $ℐ⁺$, the radiative degrees of freedom lie along the transverse $l$ congruence. On $ℐ⁻$ the transverse congruence is instead $n$, and the relevant shear is a different Newman–Penrose spin coefficient, $λ$:

\[λ = c_λ m̄^a m̄^b ∇_a n_b,\]

which has spin weight $-2$. This is exactly analogous to the Weyl tower: just as the radiative Weyl component switches from the outgoing $ψ₄$ on $ℐ⁺$ to the incoming $ψ₀$ on $ℐ⁻$, the radiative shear switches from $σ$ to $λ$. The conversion factor for $λ$ follows the same logic:

\[{}^{[A]}λ = \frac{c_s c_λ}{c_l c_m^2}\, λ.\]

In the default convention, both radiative shears are tied to the strain. The relation follows from the definitions above. At $ℐ⁺$ the conformal angular metric is $h_{AB} = γ_{AB} + ω\,C_{AB} + …$ and the cross term of the conformal metric is $ĝ_{uω} = 1$, so the only Christoffel symbol needed is

\[Γ̂^u{}_{AB} = -\frac{1}{2} ∂_ω h_{AB} = -\frac{C_{AB}}{2}.\]

Contracting it with the lowered leg $l̃_a = -c_s c_l (du)_a/\sqrt{2}$ of the previous page, and with the dyad twice, gives

\[σ = -c_σ\, m̃^a m̃^b ∇̂_a l̃_b = -c_σ\, \frac{c_s c_l}{\sqrt{2}}\, m̃^A m̃^B Γ̂^u{}_{AB} = c_s c_σ c_l c_m^2\, \frac{J^{(1)}}{2\sqrt{2}}, \qquad J^{(1)} = \frac{1}{2} C_{AB}\, q^A q^B,\]

the prefactor being exactly the conversion factor derived above, as it must be. Here $J^{(1)}$ is the leading coefficient of the Bondi metric function $J$ of Moxon et al. [3], and it equals $h̄$ at the default $c_h = 1$ by the strain expression given earlier. The same computation at $ℐ⁻$, with $ñ$ in place of $l̃$, gives the conjugate relation:

\[σ = \frac{h̄}{2\sqrt2} \quad (ℐ⁺), \qquad λ = \frac{h}{2\sqrt2} \quad (ℐ⁻),\]

with all three understood as the finite radiative data described at the top of this page. This agrees with Eq. (86c) of Moxon et al. [3] in the limit. That equation gives the shear at finite radius, in terms of the same $J$ and of $K = \sqrt{1 + J J̄}$, as

\[σ = -\frac{(1 + K)\, ∂_r J}{4 \sqrt{2}\, K} + \frac{J^2\, ∂_r J̄}{4 \sqrt{2}\, K (1 + K)}.\]

As $r → ∞$ we have $J = J^{(1)}/r + …$, so $K → 1$ and the second term falls off like $r^{-4}$, leaving only the first. There $∂_r J = -J^{(1)}/r^2 + …$, whose sign cancels the one in front, and $(1 + K)/K → 2$ turns the $1/4\sqrt{2}$ into $1/2\sqrt{2}$:

\[σ = \frac{J^{(1)}}{2 \sqrt{2}\, r^2} + O(r^{-3}),\]

which is the rescaled value above.

The dyad is null only with respect to its own metric

The round dyad used above is null at $ℐ$, where the conformal angular metric is the unit sphere, but it is not null on the perturbed metric at finite radius, where $m ⋅ m = c_m^2 J^{(1)}/r$. Repeating the computation there — with the physical metric, the physical connection, and $r^2 h_{AB}$ in place of $h_{AB}$ — therefore requires the correction that restores nullity, which is written out in Eq. (81a) of Moxon et al. [3] and reads $m^A = c_m(q^A - J q̄^A/2)/ \sqrt{2} r$ to first order in $J$. That correction is not a small one: it contributes $-4J^{(1)}$ against the $+2J^{(1)}$ of the term that survives without it, so omitting it reverses the sign of the relation between the shear and the strain.

  • 1Note that defining $h$ this way forces it to have spin weight $-2$, which is nearly universal in the modern GW astronomy community. There are sources that define the strain to have spin weight $+2$, which is equivalent to taking the complex conjugate of our definition. That choice cannot be accommodated automatically by this package.

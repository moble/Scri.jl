Computing $ðt'/2κ$

The combination $ðt'/2κ$ (where $t$ can represent either $u$ or $v$) appears in the BMS transformation of the Weyl components. Thus, computing it accurately and efficiently is a key part of the BMS pipeline.

We have the coordinate transformation

\[\begin{gather} t' = κ(t - c_α α), \\ \frac{1}{κ} = γ(1 - ℐv⃗⋅n̂), \end{gather}\]

where $c_α$ accounts for differences in the definition of the supertranslation parameter $α$, and $ℐ$ accounts for whether we're dealing with $ℐ⁺$ ($ℐ = +1$) or $ℐ⁻$ ($ℐ = -1$).

Note that $1/κ$ has a simple form — and specifically, it decomposes into a pure $ℓ = 0$ plus a pure $ℓ = 1$ function on the sphere. This allows us to compute $ð(1/κ)$ in closed form, without needing to evaluate the synthesis sum at all. The $ð$ operator is a derivation, meaning that it obeys the Leibniz rule, so as usual we have $ð(κ) = -ð(1/κ) κ^2$.

\[\begin{align} \frac{ðt'}{2κ} &= \frac{ð(κ)}{2κ} (t - c_α α) - \frac{c_α ðα}{2} \\ &= \frac{-ð(1/κ)}{2}κ (t - c_α α) - \frac{c_α ðα}{2} \\ &= \frac{ℐ ð(v⃗⋅n̂)}{2(1 - ℐv⃗⋅n̂)} (t - c_α α) - \frac{c_α ðα}{2} \\ &= \frac{-ð(v⃗⋅n̂)}{2(v⃗⋅n̂-ℐ)} (t - c_α α) - \frac{c_α ðα}{2}. \end{align}\]

Since $α$ and $ðα$ may contain — in principle — arbitrarily high $ℓ$ modes, it will be best to compute them via SphericalFunctions.jl. However, the $v⃗⋅n̂$ and $ð(v⃗⋅n̂)$ terms are simple enough that it will be more efficient to compute them directly in closed form, using rotor components. That is the objective of what follows.

$v⃗⋅n̂$ as a function on $\mathrm{Spin}(3)$

As mentioned when we introduced the $ð$ operator, $ð$ is best understood as a derivative operator on the group $\mathrm{Spin}(3)$. It is not immediately obvious that $v⃗⋅n̂$ needs to be expressed as a function on $\mathrm{Spin}(3)$, but in order to obtain an expression for $ðt'/2κ$ that is a function on $\mathrm{Spin}(3)$, we need to express all of the terms in that form. The solution is simple:

\[v⃗⋅n̂ : Q ↦ v⃗ ⋅ (Q ẑ Q̄) = (Q̄ v⃗ Q) ⋅ ẑ.\]

That is, $Q$ is interpreted as a rotation that takes the $ẑ$ basis vector to the direction $n̂$, and the inner product is taken with $v⃗$. Note the equivalent form in the final expression, which will be make the final result more efficient to calculate. In fact, we will define

\[\vec{λ} = Q̄ v⃗ Q,\]

which can be calculated once, very efficiently. Then we just need to take the $ẑ$ component of $\vec{λ}$ to get $v⃗⋅n̂ = λᶻ$, and we will see that its other components are exactly what we need to compute $ð(v⃗⋅n̂)$.

Computing $ð(v⃗⋅n̂)$

We can now immediately apply the definition of $ð$ to compute $ð(v⃗⋅n̂)$. We have

\[\begin{aligned} ð(v⃗⋅n̂) &= c_ð R_{x̂+iŷ} v⃗⋅n̂ \\ &= -c_ð i \left.\frac{d}{dϵ}\right|_{ϵ=0} v⃗ ⋅ (Q e^{-ϵ(x̂+iŷ)/2} ẑ e^{ϵ(x̂+iŷ)/2} Q̄) \\ &= -c_ð i v⃗ ⋅ \left[Q \left( -(x̂+iŷ)/2 ẑ + ẑ (x̂+iŷ)/2 \right) Q̄\right] \\ &= c_ð v⃗ ⋅ \left[Q \left( (ix̂-ŷ) ẑ \right) Q̄\right] \\ &= c_ð v⃗ ⋅ \left[Q \left( -iŷ-x̂ \right) Q̄\right] \\ &= c_ð \left( Q̄ v⃗ Q \right) ⋅ \left( -iŷ-x̂ \right) \\ &= -c_ð \left( λˣ + i λʸ \right). \end{aligned}\]

Putting it together

The final result is simple to calculate:

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{ðt'}{2κ} &= \frac{-ð(v⃗⋅n̂)}{2(v⃗⋅n̂-ℐ)} (t - c_α α) - \frac{c_α ðα}{2} \\ &= c_ð \frac{λˣ + i λʸ}{2(λᶻ-ℐ)} (t - c_α α) - \frac{c_α ðα}{2}. \end{aligned}\]

The term proportional to $t$ will vary between time steps, so we factor out a term constant in time and one proportional to time:

\[\frac{ðt'}{2κ} = \left(\frac{ðt'}{2κ}\right)_0 + \left(\frac{ðt'}{2κ}\right)_1 t,\]

where

\[\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{ðt'}{2κ}\right)_0 &= -c_α \left( c_ð \frac{λˣ + i λʸ}{2(λᶻ-ℐ)} α + ðα \right), \\ \left(\frac{ðt'}{2κ}\right)_1 &= c_ð \frac{λˣ + i λʸ}{2(λᶻ-ℐ)}. \end{aligned}\]
